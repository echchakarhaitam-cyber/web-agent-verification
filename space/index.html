Can you trust an AI agent when it says "done"?

An AI agent that works on websites for you ends each task with a report: done or not done. If you trust that report and it is wrong, the error goes unnoticed. This study measured how often small local agents (Qwen2.5 0.5B and 1.5B) claim success when the task was not done, and whether cheap signals can tell which reports to double-check.

This page replays the 600 recorded episodes (the models do not run here). Every page was rebuilt from its seed and the agent's recorded answers applied again: all episodes reproduce the recorded outcome exactly. Code, protocol and analysis: github.com/echchakarhaitam-cyber/web-agent-verification.

Why it matters

The risk

An agent books, fills in or submits something and says "done". Nobody checks a task reported as done, so a false "done" is the costly error.

What was measured

On 15 web tasks × 20 random versions × 2 models, the agent's report was compared with the page's own record of success, and four signals (its confidence, its token probabilities, a second-opinion judge) were tested as alarms.

What it means

Even the larger model's "done" is often wrong, and none of the signals was reliable enough to decide automatically which reports to trust: with agents this small, a person must check. The method said so instead of giving a false guarantee.
